## LinkedIn

Post the image with this text. Put the link in the first comment.

```
Diesel reached $6.529 a gallon the week of September 21, the highest weekly price in the federal series that began in 1994, before adjusting for inflation. A year ago it was $3.749.

Plenty of coverage has told you that costs everyone money. We wanted to know how much, and where it lands. So we built a free calculator that follows one diesel price through the whole chain.

At $6.53, the fuel surcharge is about a quarter of a truckload freight bill, and the whole freight bill is up about 15% from a year ago.

Less of that reaches you than you might think, because freight is a small part of what a product costs. Across all goods it is about 4.7% of the shelf price, which works out to 0.7% to 1.1% on goods prices.

Groceries are the exception. Food is heavy, cheap by the pound and moves through more steps, so freight is about 9.6% of what groceries cost on the shelf. That puts grocery prices about 1.5% to 1.7% higher, if the whole increase is passed along.

For anyone watching the government's inflation data: through freight alone, this could add up to 0.44 percentage points to the CPI. That is a one-time step in the price level rather than a new ongoing rate, and it does not count what people pay at the pump.

One thing that surprised us. Published surcharge tables are much higher than what carriers actually collect. At this price Old Dominion's published table says 55.8%, while LTL carriers collect about 35% of their base charges, measured from their own filings.

The calculator is free, the method is written out step by step, and the code and data are public.
```
